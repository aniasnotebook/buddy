# How people learn: rules for every session

Each rule is from research; "inference" marks the team's own reading.

1. **Retrieve, never reread.** Every exercise is answered before the answer shows. Testing beat restudying at two days and one week. (Roediger & Karpicke 2006, doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)
2. **She commits before she sees.** She taps an answer first. (inference from rule 1)
3. **Space it.** Shaky items come back next session, then a few days later, then later still; the gap grows with the time left before the exam. (Cepeda et al. 2006, doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354; exact gaps are inference)
4. **Mix look-alike problems** so she must first pick the method (break-even vs return on investment vs variance). Keep pure definitions in blocks. (Brunmair & Richter 2019, doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209)
5. **Calculations: worked, then faded.** A full worked solution, then the last step blank, then two blank, then none. Ask her to name the rule at each step. (Renkl & Atkinson 2003, doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3)
6. **Ask why.** "Why is a new competitor a threat, not a weakness?" (Bisra et al. 2018, doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)
7. **Test application, not just recall.** A four-line case: which idea from her course applies? (Pan & Rickard 2018, doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151)
8. **Hard but doable.** After two misses in a row, drop to a worked example, then come back. (inference)
9. **Missed items come back changed:** new numbers, or the step blanked. (Butler & Roediger 2008, doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604)
10. **Match the kinds of thinking to her exam:** remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate. (Krathwohl 2002)
11. **One idea per session,** 5 to 8 exercises, no setup. (inference: ten minutes on a phone)

## Exam format to exercise

- Multiple choice: choice questions with plausible wrong options and feedback. (Little et al. 2012, doi.org/10.1177/0956797612443370)
- Calculation problems: worked examples that fade, then mixed problems.
- Case studies: short cases, "which idea applies, and why".
- Essays: recall the framework from memory, compare with a model answer, justify each point. (inference)

## Wrong options and feedback

- Wrong options are plausible mistakes from her course (fixed vs variable cost, strength vs opportunity), never opposites or negations. Three wrong options are enough. (Haladyna et al. 2002)
- Feedback right after each answer: why the answer is right, and why her choice tempted her. Task-focused, no praise. (Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2019, doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087)

## Never

Rereading, highlighting or summarising as exercises: low value. (Dunlosky et al. 2013, doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)
