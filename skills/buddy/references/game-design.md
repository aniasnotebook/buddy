# Game design: rules for every session

The fun is the learning: every card makes her spot or use a pattern from her course. Gamification helps knowledge a little (g = 0.49) and no single element reliably drives it, so the learning design carries the round. (Sailer & Homner 2020)

1. **Fun is a pattern clicking.** Each card teaches the pattern behind it. (Koster, A Theory of Fun)
2. **Start easy, then ramp.** The first card she gets right; then more ambiguous cases. Flow needs a clear goal, instant feedback and challenge matched to skill.
3. **Feedback in under a second, with the reason.** (Brilliant's teaching approach)
4. **One small idea per card, and she decides.** A six-step method is six cards.
5. **Misses come back,** a few cards later, then next session. (Settles & Meeder 2016, Duolingo)
6. **She chooses:** the part, the goal, swipe "knew it / review" on recall cards. Feeling in control predicts enjoyment and coming back. (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006)
7. **Progress counts learning, not attendance:** "5 of 8 steps solid", never "day 12".
8. **Reward only mastery.** No points for showing up; badges and leaderboards lowered motivation and scores in a class study. (Hanus & Fox 2015)
9. **A little juice on right answers only,** under half a second. (inference on timing)
10. **End on what she now knows, plus one hook:** "Steps 1 to 3 solid. Step 4 next time."

## The round

1. One-line goal ("Sort 6 cases into SWOT boxes").
2. A warm-up card she gets right.
3. Harder cards, each answered instantly with the reason and the source line.
4. Misses return a few cards later.
5. The end: solid, shaky, next time, and "Copy my results".
